Why the direction changes

East has higher known-outcome success rates for both services. Assisted exceeds standard in East (80.0% vs 72.0%, +8.0 pp) and West (60.0% vs 40.0%, +20.0 pp). Yet only 16.7% of assisted known cases are in East, versus 83.3% of standard known cases (denominator: each service’s known cases). Assisted is concentrated in the lower-success branch, producing a Simpson’s-paradox reversal.

The equal-branch result averages East and West rates with weight 0.5 each within each service. It answers a hypothetical equal-branch-mix question, not the result for the observed case mix or a causal effect.

Unknown outcomes and evidence limits

20 of 140 cases have unknown outcomes (14.3% of all cases). Each service has 10 unknowns out of 70 cases (14.3% of all cases per service), but missingness varies sharply by branch: standard West has 9/19 unknown (47.4% of its cases). Blank outcomes are excluded only from success-rate denominators, never counted as failures or imputed. If missingness relates to success, even the within-branch comparisons can be biased.

This observational log does not establish causation: service assignment was not randomized, branch adjustment does not control severity or other unrecorded differences, and follow-up may differ. These are descriptive estimates for this pilot; no confidence intervals, significance tests, or population-level claims are made.

Practical next step

Recover unknown outcomes, especially standard West, and audit assignment, severity and follow-up consistency. Before scaling, run a prospectively planned pilot with random assignment within branch where feasible, a common outcome window and complete follow-up. Use these results to justify further evaluation, not an immediate causal performance claim.
