Cedar pilot / Operations evidence brief

Assisted handling: better within branches, not in the pooled log

Bottom line: assisted has higher observed success rates in both East and West, but a lower pooled rate. The comparison changes direction when each branch gets equal weight. This is descriptive evidence, not proof that assisted handling causes better outcomes.

What changes the answer?

East: assisted 8/10 = 80.0%, standard 36/50 = 72.0% (+8.0 pp). West: assisted 30/50 = 60.0%, standard 4/10 = 40.0% (+20.0 pp). All four rates divide successes by known outcomes.

Among known outcomes, assisted is concentrated in lower-success West (50/60 = 83.3% of its known cases), while standard is concentrated in higher-success East (50/60 = 83.3% of its known cases). This mix produces a Simpson’s-paradox reversal: pooled assisted looks worse even though it looks better in each branch.

The equal-branch calculation is (80.0% + 60.0%)/2 = 70.0% for assisted and (72.0% + 40.0%)/2 = 56.0% for standard. Its 14.0 pp difference describes a hypothetical 50/50 branch mix, not the actual overall case mix or a causal effect. It adjusts only for branch.

There are 20 unknown outcomes among 140 cases (14.3% of all cases). Each service has 10/70 unknown (14.3%), but missingness differs sharply by branch: assisted East 4/14 (28.6%) and West 6/56 (10.7%); standard East 1/51 (2.0%) and West 9/19 (47.4%). Equal service-level missingness does not remove this concern.

Blank outcomes remain unknown and are excluded from success-rate denominators, never coded as failures. If missing outcomes differ systematically from recorded ones, both comparisons may be biased.

Decision and next step

Do not declare a winner from this log alone. Assignment was not randomized; case difficulty, routing, staffing, timing or other unrecorded factors may differ even within a branch. One row per case does not establish independence across staff or repeated customers. These are point estimates for the observed log; no sampling-based confidence or significance claim is made.

Next: recover unknown outcomes first, especially standard West, and audit routing and case difficulty by branch. If operationally feasible, run a prospective randomized comparison within each branch, with a fixed follow-up window, consistent outcome capture, and a prespecified weighting rule. Until then, use these results as a signal to investigate, not a basis for a causal rollout claim.
