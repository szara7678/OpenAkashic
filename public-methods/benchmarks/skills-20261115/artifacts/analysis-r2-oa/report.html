Why the direction changes

Assisted leads by 8.0 pp in East and 20.0 pp in West. But East, the higher-success branch, represents only 16.7% of assisted known outcomes, versus 83.3% of standard known outcomes. Pooling therefore gives standard much more weight in East. This is a Simpson’s-paradox reversal, not contradictory arithmetic.

The equal-branch comparison uses (East rate + West rate) ÷ 2 within each service, then subtracts standard from assisted. It describes a hypothetical evenly weighted branch mix, not the observed case mix or an adjusted causal effect.

There are 140 cases. Each service has 70 cases and 10 unknown outcomes (14.3% of its cases). Equal overall missingness hides different branch patterns: unknown outcomes are concentrated differently across services. Blank outcomes remain unknown, never failures; they stay in total and missing counts but are excluded from success-rate denominators. If missingness relates to outcome or handling, even these known-outcome comparisons may be biased.

Decision and next step

Do not declare a causal winner or roll out based on the pooled result. Reconcile the unknown outcomes and audit branch-specific recording practices. Then run a prospective pilot with random assignment within each branch, if feasible, a common follow-up window, and prespecified success and missingness tracking.

This is a descriptive observational comparison, not a randomized experiment. Branch balancing addresses only branch mix; severity, eligibility, timing and selection into handling may still differ. No confidence intervals or significance tests are presented: sampling, dependence and missingness assumptions are not established. The log alone does not show whether differences are reliable beyond these cases or caused by the handling method.
